**This Agreement** is made between:

**Employer:** Lulu Island Flagship Cleaning Services Inc., a corporation incorporated under the laws of British Columbia, with its registered office in Richmond, B.C. ("the Company")

**Employee:** `[EMPLOYEE_FULL_NAME]`, residing at `[EMPLOYEE_ADDRESS]` ("the Employee")

(Collectively "the Parties")

---

## 1. POSITION AND START DATE

1.1 **Position.** The Employee is hired as a `[POSITION_TITLE]` (e.g., Cleaning Technician, Team Lead, Supervisor).

1.2 **Start Date.** Employment begins on `[START_DATE]`.

1.3 **Probationary Period.** The first three (3) months of employment constitute a probationary period. During this period, either Party may terminate this Agreement with one (1) week's written notice or pay in lieu thereof.

1.4 **Reporting To.** The Employee reports to `[SUPERVISOR_NAME]`, `[SUPERVISOR_TITLE]`.

---

## 2. REMUNERATION

2.1 **Day Rate.** The Employee is compensated on a day-rate basis (not hourly), consistent with the Company's operational model. The initial day rate is `$[DAY_RATE]` CAD per full workday.

2.2 **Pay Period.** Wages are paid semi-monthly (15th and last day of each month) via direct deposit to the bank account provided by the Employee.

2.3 **Wage Statement.** Each pay shall be accompanied by a wage statement detailing gross wages, deductions, and net pay, in compliance with section 27 of the *Employment Standards Act* (BC).

2.4 **Annual Review.** The day rate is reviewed annually on the anniversary of the Start Date. Adjustments, if any, consider the British Columbia Consumer Price Index (CPI) and individual performance. Any adjustment is at the Company's sole discretion unless otherwise required by law.

---

## 3. HOURS OF WORK

3.1 **Workday.** A standard workday is `[HOURS_PER_DAY]` hours, typically between `[START_TIME]` and `[END_TIME]`, Monday through Friday. Actual start and end times may vary based on client scheduling and travel between service locations.

3.2 **Meal Breaks.** The Employee is entitled to an unpaid 30-minute meal break after every five (5) consecutive hours of work, as required by section 32 of the *Employment Standards Act* (BC).

3.3 **Minimum Daily Hours.** If the Employee reports to work as scheduled and work is not available, the Employee shall be paid for a minimum of two (2) hours at the applicable day-rate proportion, per section 34 of the *Employment Standards Act* (BC).

3.4 **Hours Free From Work.** The Employee shall have at least eight (8) consecutive hours free from work between shifts, per section 36 of the *Employment Standards Act* (BC).

3.5 **Split Shifts.** If the Employee's schedule includes a split shift, the second portion shall not start later than 12 hours after the first portion began, per section 33 of the *Employment Standards Act* (BC).

3.6 **No Excessive Hours.** The Employee shall not be required or permitted to work excessive hours that could endanger health or safety. The system (`workday.ts`, `shift-rest.ts`) enforces schedule compliance.

---

## 4. OVERTIME

4.1 **Overtime Rates.** Overtime is payable as follows, per sections 35–40 of the *Employment Standards Act* (BC):

- **1.5×** the regular day-rate proportion for hours worked over eight (8) in a day or over forty (40) in a week.
- **2×** the regular day-rate proportion for hours worked over twelve (12) in a day.

4.2 **Overtime Authorization.** All overtime must be pre-authorized in writing (including via the PWA scheduling system) by the Employee's supervisor.

4.3 **Averaging Agreements.** The Company may propose an averaging agreement under section 37 of the *Employment Standards Act* (BC). Any such agreement is voluntary and must be signed by both Parties.

4.4 **Banking of Overtime.** By mutual written agreement, overtime hours may be banked at the applicable overtime rate and taken as paid time off within six (6) months, per section 42 of the *Employment Standards Act* (BC).

---

## 5. STATUTORY HOLIDAYS

5.1 **Recognized Holidays.** The Company observes the eleven (11) statutory holidays of British Columbia: New Year's Day, Family Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day, Canada Day, B.C. Day, Labour Day, Thanksgiving Day, Remembrance Day, Christmas Day, and Boxing Day (as defined in `statutory-holidays.ts`).

5.2 **Holiday Pay.** An eligible Employee who does not work on a statutory holiday receives an average day's pay. An eligible Employee who works on a statutory holiday receives 1.5× the regular rate for hours worked, plus an average day's pay, per Part 5 of the *Employment Standards Act* (BC).

5.3 **Eligibility.** The Employee must have been employed for at least thirty (30) calendar days and must have worked or earned wages on at least fifteen (15) of the thirty (30) days preceding the holiday.

---

## 6. VACATION

6.1 **Annual Entitlement.** The Employee is entitled to:

- **Two (2) weeks** of vacation after each of the first five (5) years of employment (vacation pay at 4% of gross wages).
- **Three (3) weeks** of vacation after five (5) consecutive years of employment (vacation pay at 6% of gross wages).

6.2 **Vacation Pay.** Vacation pay is accrued on all wages and may be paid on each paycheque or taken as paid time off, at the Employee's election communicated in writing.

6.3 **Scheduling.** Vacation must be requested at least four (4) weeks in advance and is subject to operational requirements. The Company shall not unreasonably withhold approval.

---

## 7. SICK LEAVE AND OTHER LEAVES

7.1 **Paid Sick Leave.** The Employee is entitled to five (5) paid sick days and three (3) unpaid sick days per employment year, as provided by the *Employment Standards Act* (BC) and managed by the system (`sick-leave.ts`).

7.2 **Other Statutory Leaves.** The Employee is entitled to all leaves protected under the *Employment Standards Act* (BC), including but not limited to: maternity leave, parental leave, family responsibility leave, bereavement leave, and reservist leave. The applicable notice and documentation requirements of the Act apply.

7.3 **Workplace Injury or Illness.** In the event of a work-related injury or illness, the Employee shall immediately report the incident through the PWA (`workplace-incident.ts`) and the Company shall file the appropriate report with WorkSafeBC.

---

## 8. WORKSAFEBC COVERAGE

8.1 **Registration.** The Company is registered with WorkSafeBC under registration number `[WORKSAFEBC_NUMBER]`. The Employee is covered for work-related injuries and occupational diseases under the *Workers Compensation Act* (BC).

8.2 **Incident Reporting.** Any workplace injury, no matter how minor, must be reported immediately via the PWA incident reporting system and to the Employee's supervisor. The Company shall file Form 7 (Employer's Report of Injury or Occupational Disease) with WorkSafeBC within the statutory deadline.

8.3 **Right to Refuse Unsafe Work.** The Employee has the right to refuse work that poses an undue hazard to health or safety, under the OHS provisions of the *Workers Compensation Act* (BC), without reprisal.

---

## 9. NON-DISCRIMINATION

9.1 **Commitment.** The Company is committed to providing a workplace free from discrimination and harassment, in compliance with the *Human Rights Code* (BC). The Company does not discriminate on the basis of race, colour, ancestry, place of origin, religion, marital status, family status, physical or mental disability, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, age, political belief, or criminal conviction unrelated to employment.

9.2 **Equal Pay.** The Company shall not pay an Employee of one sex at a lower rate than an Employee of another sex for substantially similar work, per section 12 of the *Human Rights Code* (BC).

9.3 **Complaint Procedure.** Any Employee who believes they have experienced discrimination or harassment shall report the matter to `[HR_CONTACT]`. All reports shall be investigated promptly and confidentially. No retaliation shall be taken against any Employee for making a good-faith report.

---

## 10. PRIVACY AND PERSONAL INFORMATION

10.1 **Collection of Personal Information.** The Company collects the Employee's personal information for the following purposes only: payroll administration, tax remittance, scheduling, WorkSafeBC compliance, benefits administration, performance management, and emergency contact. This is in compliance with the *Personal Information Protection Act* (BC) ("PIPA").

10.2 **Sensitive Information.** The Employee's Social Insurance Number (SIN), banking information, and emergency contact details are encrypted at rest (`crypto.server.ts`) and used solely for the purposes stated above.

10.3 **No Sale or Unauthorized Disclosure.** The Company does not sell, rent, or disclose Employee personal information to third parties except as required by law (e.g., CRA for tax purposes, WorkSafeBC for claims).

10.4 **Access and Correction.** The Employee may request access to or correction of their personal information by contacting `[PRIVACY_OFFICER]`. The Company shall respond within thirty (30) days.

10.5 **Retention.** Employee personal information is retained for the period required by applicable law (typically seven (7) years after the employment relationship ends for payroll and tax records) and securely destroyed thereafter.

---

## 11. TAX AND SOURCE DEDUCTIONS

11.1 **Authorization to Deduct.** The Employee authorizes the Company to deduct from wages the required amounts for:

- Canada Pension Plan (CPP) contributions
- Employment Insurance (EI) premiums
- Federal and provincial income tax

as mandated by the *Income Tax Act* (Canada) and the *Canada Pension Plan Act*.

11.2 **TD1 Forms.** The Employee shall complete federal and provincial TD1 (Personal Tax Credits Return) forms before the first pay period and shall update them within seven (7) days of any change affecting tax credits.

11.3 **SIN Disclosure.** The Employee shall provide their valid Social Insurance Number (SIN) on or before the Start Date. Failure to provide a valid SIN may delay or prevent payroll processing.

---

## 12. DUTIES AND CONDUCT

12.1 **Core Duties.** The Employee's core duties include but are not limited to:

- Performing cleaning services according to the Company's Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) by zone
- Following the chemical lockout protocol (`chemical-lockout.ts`): using only Company-approved products and concentrations
- Wearing required Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) at all times during service
- Taking timestamped completion photos of each serviced zone through the PWA
- Participating in pre-service briefing and end-of-shift ritual (`shift-ritual.ts`)
- Maintaining the Company's equipment and vehicle in clean, operational condition
- Reporting any client concern, incident, or damage immediately via the PWA

12.2 **Chemical and Safety Protocol.** The Employee shall adhere strictly to the Company's chemical lockout system. The Employee shall not use any cleaning product not explicitly approved in the system. Safety Data Sheets (SDS) for all approved products are available in the PWA.

12.3 **Key Handling.** If the Employee is entrusted with client keys or alarm codes, the Employee must follow the key handling protocol (`key-handling.ts`). Lost keys or security breaches must be reported within thirty (30) minutes of discovery. Keys and codes are stored and transmitted only through the encrypted Company system (`crypto.ts`).

12.4 **Photo Evidence.** The Employee shall capture and upload timestamped, geotagged photos of each zone after completion. These photos serve as the Company's primary quality assurance and dispute resolution evidence (Invariant #6: Evidence over Opinion).

12.5 **Client Confidentiality.** The Employee shall not disclose any information about the Company's clients — including addresses, property details, security information, or personal circumstances — to any third party, during or after employment. This clause survives termination of this Agreement.

12.6 **No Independent Work.** During the term of this Agreement, the Employee shall not perform cleaning services directly for any Company client outside of the Company's booking and dispatch system.

---

## 13. EQUIPMENT, VEHICLE, AND UNIFORM

13.1 **Company Equipment.** The Company provides all cleaning equipment, chemicals, PPE, and PWA-enabled devices. The Employee is responsible for the reasonable care and safekeeping of all Company equipment.

13.2 **Company Vehicle.** If assigned a Company vehicle for work-related travel, the Employee must hold a valid BC driver's licence and maintain a clean driving record. The vehicle is for work use only, unless otherwise authorized in writing. The Employee shall report any damage, accident, or mechanical issue immediately.

13.3 **Uniform.** The Employee shall wear the Company uniform during all service appointments and maintain it in clean, professional condition.

---

## 14. TERMINATION OF EMPLOYMENT

14.1 **Termination by the Company (Without Just Cause).** The Company may terminate this Agreement without just cause by providing the following notice or pay in lieu thereof, per section 63 of the *Employment Standards Act* (BC):

| Length of Employment | Notice or Pay in Lieu |
|---|---|
| Less than 3 months | None (probation: 1 week per section 1.3 of this Agreement) |
| 3 months to 1 year | 1 week |
| 1 to 3 years | 2 weeks |
| 3 to 5 years | 3 weeks |
| 5 to 10 years | 4 weeks |
| 10+ years | 5 to 8 weeks (per ESA formula) |

14.2 **Termination for Just Cause.** The Company may terminate this Agreement immediately without notice or pay in lieu for just cause, including but not limited to: theft, fraud, gross insubordination, wilful misconduct, breach of confidentiality, violation of the chemical lockout or safety protocol, or abandonment of position.

14.3 **Termination by the Employee.** The Employee may resign by providing the following written notice, per section 63 of the *Employment Standards Act* (BC):

- One (1) week, if employed less than two (2) years
- Two (2) weeks, if employed two (2) years or more

14.4 **Final Pay.** Upon termination, the Company shall pay all outstanding wages, accrued vacation pay, and any other amounts owing within the time required by section 18 of the *Employment Standards Act* (BC).

14.5 **Return of Property.** Upon termination, the Employee shall immediately return all Company property — including keys, access cards, uniforms, devices, equipment, and any documents (physical or digital) — in good condition, reasonable wear excepted.

## 15. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

15.1 **Company IP.** All materials, systems, methods, checklists, SOPs, pricing data, client lists, and any other work product created or accessed during employment are and remain the sole property of the Company.

15.2 **Return on Termination.** The Employee retains no right, title, or interest in any Company intellectual property after termination.

## 16. DISPUTE RESOLUTION

16.1 **Internal Resolution.** The Parties shall attempt to resolve any dispute arising from this Agreement through direct discussion between the Employee and `[HR_CONTACT]` or the Company's designated representative.

16.2 **Mediation.** If direct resolution fails, the Parties agree to participate in mediation facilitated by a neutral third party before pursuing any other remedy.

16.3 **Governing Law.** This Agreement is governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the Province of British Columbia and the applicable federal laws of Canada. The courts of British Columbia have exclusive jurisdiction.

---

## 17. ELECTRONIC SIGNATURE AND VERSIONING

17.1 **Electronic Signature.** The Parties agree that this Agreement may be executed by electronic signature, which shall have the same legal effect as a handwritten signature. The Company uses a PIPEDA-compliant e-signature provider (`esignature-provider.ts`).

17.2 **Version Control.** This Agreement exists in the Company's contract management system with full version history. Each year, on or before the anniversary of the Start Date, the Agreement shall be reviewed (`contract-review.ts`). If changes are required by law, a new version shall be generated, and the previous version shall be marked "superseded" (never deleted). A change log summarizing differences is available in the system.

17.3 **Annual IPC Adjustment.** The Company may adjust the day rate annually based on the BC Consumer Price Index. Any such adjustment shall be communicated in writing at least thirty (30) days before the effective date (`contract-ipc-adjustment.ts`).

## 18. GENERAL PROVISIONS

18.1 **Entire Agreement.** This Agreement constitutes the entire agreement between the Parties with respect to the employment relationship and supersedes all prior discussions, representations, and agreements, whether written or oral.

18.2 **No Waiver.** The failure of either Party to enforce any provision of this Agreement shall not constitute a waiver of that provision or of any other provision.

18.3 **Severability.** If any provision of this Agreement is held invalid or unenforceable by a court of competent jurisdiction, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force and effect, and the invalid provision shall be modified to the minimum extent necessary to make it valid and enforceable.

18.4 **Amendments.** No amendment to this Agreement shall be effective unless in writing and signed (including electronically) by both Parties, or as permitted under sections 17.2–17.3 of this Agreement.

18.5 **Survival.** Sections 12.5 (Client Confidentiality), 15 (Intellectual Property), 16 (Dispute Resolution), and 14.5 (Return of Property) survive termination of this Agreement.

18.6 **Language.** This Agreement is provided in both English and French. In the event of any discrepancy, the English version shall prevail to the extent permitted by applicable law.
